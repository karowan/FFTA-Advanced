# Shared native anchors for portrait and badge review

The user rejected the round-three badge directions and facial drift. All ten
badges must face directly forward. Human Dark Knight portrait eyes are two
simple golden circles inside helmet darkness, with no human face or nose.
Viera Mystic Knight needs coherent eyes and a visible mouth.

[The complete round-four review](../build/art/job-art-approval-v4-2026-09-20/index.html)
includes ten new frontal badges, the two revised portraits, first-batch badge
comparisons, original race icons, extracted facial anchors and all existing
animations, ordered keyframes and game screenshots. Use Codex browser comments;
there is no custom annotation system. New artwork remains **unapproved and not
imported**. Screenshots show the preceding assembled build, not these proposals.

## Reusable process

1. Decode clean original native job badges; crop the head at `(1,1,17,15)`.
   Do not use dimmed equipment screenshots. Keep their indexed pixels so native
   facial color roles can be compared across existing palette banks.
2. Extract agreement inside an explicitly bounded facial region. Keep costume,
   frame and background pixels out of the mask. Record reference jobs, coordinates
   and original indices. A mask is shared by the listed references, not asserted
   to be universal across every job or covered-face variant.
3. Generate one new job at a time in a five-head row: four original reference
   heads and a fifth structural template. Include the approved concept,
   first sprite-based head and extracted anchor layer. Require direct frontal
   facing, the same eye columns, head center and chin baseline. New costume art
   is model-generated; the template supplies anatomy, not the character design.
4. Preserve the raw output. Inspect which cell actually changed. Record any
   layout deviation rather than silently extracting the wrong cell. These
   outputs added transparent letterboxing: the recorded strip bounds are the
   span of rows with over 98% opaque pixels. Sample that strip to 80x14 and crop
   the recorded 16x14 cell. Do not fit individual heads to new bounding boxes.
5. Map to an existing native palette, then restore only extracted shared native
   facial pixels for uncovered faces. No new pixel shapes are hand-painted or
   generated by code. Inspect the surrounding generated face for extra features;
   an anchor match alone does not establish good art.
6. Closed helmets have their own rule: no skin-mask compositing. Generate their
   eyes at native columns 7 and 10 and validate the resulting golden pixels.
   If positions fail, regenerate the localized feature. Human eyes occupy rows
   7–8; Bangaa eyes occupy row 7. These coordinates are within the 16x14 head.
7. Review native-size head, full eligible/ineligible badge, original race icons
   and first-batch identity reference together. Preserve exact frame/lettering
   indices. Approval still belongs to the user before import.

Final exposed-face masks contain 19 Human, 14 Bangaa, 63 Nu Mou, 33 Moogle and
37 Viera pixels. Viera uses the three uncovered faces, jobs 28/31/33; armored job
29 remains a style reference but cannot define shared skin. Original four-job
Viera intersection (11 pixels) is preserved in the generation records. Final
conversion uses the larger uncovered-face agreement, with its own recorded
region and source jobs. This avoids retaining the generated dark patch where
the original exposed Viera face uses its skin ramp.

The standalone-head experiment still drifted in scale and placement. Its two
icon outputs were rejected. The shared-row method aligned better. Samurai and
both Dark Knight row generations initially changed the fourth rather than fifth
cell; this is explicitly recorded. Human helmet eyes needed one further edit;
Bangaa needed a covered-muzzle edit followed by a focused left-eye correction.
All raw attempts and exact prompts remain preserved.

## Native colors and portraits

No palette banks or runtime palette system were added. Dancer's badge now uses
the existing plum badge bank, like the uncovered original Viera faces, so the
native peach skin ramp survives. This changes the existing bank choice for the
offline proposal; the eligible/ineligible indices and lettering are retained.

Human Dark Knight portrait conversion uses the existing human job 10 portrait
palette (index 16): inspection across all eleven original human choices found
it retained dark armor, gold eyes and blue cloth together. The four initial
reference palettes either made the eyes pink or armor green. The whole native
48-entry palette is retained, without mixing colors from different palettes.

Portraits remain separate 48x56 proposals. They do not use the badge face mask:
original large portraits have different poses and framing. The two edited
portraits were generated with the user-provided FFT reference and original
Viera portraits respectively. Both sampled and native-palette versions remain
visible for review. Their visual acceptance is not implied by the badge checks.
